Overview

A program built around on-chain proof

Sui is secured by its validators. The more independent, well-run operators there are, the stronger and more decentralised the network becomes.

The Suinance Validator Program brings those operators together. Every rule is public, every check uses on-chain data anyone can verify, and members are introduced to the SUI holders who decide where stake goes.

What members get

Why operators join

A verified place in the directory

Members carry a Program badge on the Suinance validators page, right next to their live stake, commission and voting power.

Introductions to delegators

Suinance points its community of SUI holders to program members first, with the on-chain data they need to choose.

Checks every epoch

We review each member's on-chain record every epoch and contact the operator early when a number starts to drift.

An operator network

A private channel for member operators to share upgrade notes, incident reports and lessons from running Sui nodes.

    About the program

    What is the Suinance Validator Program?

    An independent program for Sui validator operators. Members meet public standards for commission, uptime, software and decentralisation, are checked every epoch, and are introduced to the Suinance community of SUI holders.

    Who can apply?

    Any operator running a validator on Sui mainnet with commission at or below 10% and voting power under 3%. Candidates still building stake can apply and are reviewed once they join the active set.

    Does it cost anything to join?

    No. There is no fee to apply or to stay in the program.

    How do you check validators?

    From public on-chain data: the active validator set, stake and voting power, commission, reference gas price quotes and tallying-rule reports. Anyone can verify the same numbers on a Sui explorer.

    How long does a review take?

    We look at your record over recent epochs, so a review needs a few days of on-chain history. We email you at the address on your account when it is done.

    What happens if my validator falls below a standard?

    We contact you first. You then have 7 epochs to recover. If the issue is still there, the validator leaves the program and can apply again later.

    Does Suinance hold anyone's SUI?

    No. Suinance never takes custody of SUI. Delegators stake from their own wallets, and validators keep full control of their nodes and keys.

    Is Suinance part of Sui?

    No. Suinance is not run by or affiliated with Mysten Labs or the Sui Foundation.
